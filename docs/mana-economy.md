# Mana economy — 0.45.0

Mana now belongs to the current battle. At battle start, apply opening consumable upgrades/buffs, then set mana to `min(capacity, 10 + min(10, floor(max(0, capacity - 50) / 5)))`. Add event mana, Magic-ring opening grants and Reservoir grants, respecting capacity. Neither a full nor empty pool from the last battle changes this reserve. Individual waves do not reset mana. A T1 Magic ring gives capacity 55, reserve 11 and its separate +1 opening grant: 12 total. Equipping capacity gear during preparation does not refill stored mana.

Universal generation is 0.5 mana per second rather than 1. Silence and wand suppression retain their existing rules. Robes, class effects, Insight refunds, adjacency, capacity gear and other mana generation keep their values. Magic Potions wait for a live, ready item whose actual modified mana cost exceeds current mana, and room for the potion's base refill (6 or 12). This includes weapons and off-class/enchanted costs. They do not drink simply because a battle starts below capacity, during silence, for cooling-down items or for used items. A potion later in pack order unblocks a waiting cast on the next simulation step. The displayed potion rules and preparation/event previews describe this policy.

Existing saves, gear IDs, pending paid enchantment previews and modifier values retain their formats. Loading an old run uses the new reserve on its next battle. As before, loading a saved active fight resumes in preparation.

## Controlled comparisons against 0.44.0

Results are fixed scenarios, not estimates of player win rates. Summary data: `mana-benchmark.json`.

- Resource fixture (`scripts/benchmark-mana.cjs`): 15 mana-using classes, native starter weapon/armor, 1–3 native T0 abilities and T1 Magic ring, legal placement, seed123456. Three consecutive 60-second rounds against an inert dummy, no encounter drain/suppression, incoming damage or victory rewards. No extra healing tome. 135 rounds per build. One ability: no resource delays in either build. Two abilities: 0 → 3 classes with delays (Assassin, Necromancer, Druid). Three abilities: 3 → 9 (Wizard, Priest, Paladin, Assassin, Necromancer, Huntress, Sorcerer, Summoner, Druid). All new rounds start with 12 mana in this fixture. This measures casting demand; healing/support casts may fire at full health.
- Native class fixture (`scripts/benchmark-class-identities.cjs`): 19 classes, starter/developed native gear, 4 boss cases, native ability enabled/disabled, at 1×/2×/4×. 912 fights per build. Clears: 151/304 per speed in both versions, with unchanged class totals. All 304 new cases have identical state summaries across speeds. Includes the ability-disabled ablation; it is not a 304-fight sample of normal gameplay.
- Enchantment fixture (`scripts/benchmark-enchantments.cjs`): 19 classes × 10 enchantment choices × 4 bosses × 3 speeds = 2,280 fights per build. Clears: 458/760 per speed before and after. All 760 new cases have identical outcomes, HP, time and tested activations across speeds. Existing fixture uses one native ability, duplicate native weapon, Magic/Attack rings, three Health Potions and developed gear; it does not stress multi-ability casting. See the script for exact tiers and modifiers.
- Broad tiered fixture (`scripts/benchmark-balance.cjs`): 19 classes × 35 dungeon bosses × 3 legal tiered builds = 1,995 fights per build. Clears 1,578 → 1,570: Paladin 91 → 90, Trickster 75 → 72, Druid 90 → 86; other class clear counts unchanged. This historical fixture includes an extra Healing Tome and retains that assumption for comparison.

The additional opening reserve preserves straightforward starter casting while reducing surplus regeneration and making multiple abilities compete for mana. These isolated fights do not prove full-run economy balance, and capacity-heavy UT/ST combinations are not exhaustively optimized here.
